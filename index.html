Where it goes

Hudson south across the river, west up the Clove, south through the notch, east along the reservoir, south to New Paltz, then across the Hudson.

All the climbing is in the first 28 miles. Two climbs back to back, then it is rolling with a long slow loss of elevation to the river. Feet, by mile.

If someone needs out

Four places where you can shorten the day and still get a train home. The route gets farther from a station through the middle, so decide early.

Catskill or Palenville mile 6 to 15

Turn around and ride back over the Rip Van Winkle Bridge to Hudson Amtrak. Southbound Empire Service trains run roughly every hour or two; ticket in the app.

Phoenicia mile 37

Trailways buses run down Rt 28 from Phoenicia to Kingston and on to Port Authority. Bikes go in the luggage bay if there is room, so it is a gamble. Otherwise keep going to West Hurley, the next option is better.

West Hurley mile 55

Instead of turning south, ride Rt 28 east into Kingston (about 6 mi), then over the Kingston-Rhinecliff Bridge to Rhinecliff Amtrak (about 9 mi more). That is 15 miles instead of 38. Southbound Amtrak from Rhinecliff runs through the afternoon and evening.

New Paltz mile 81

Trailways runs New Paltz to Port Authority, same bike caveat. Honestly, from here it is 11 flat miles to Poughkeepsie, so just finish.

Walkway closed mile 90

Drop down to Rt 44/55 and take the Mid-Hudson Bridge path instead. Adds about a mile and a short climb into Poughkeepsie.
